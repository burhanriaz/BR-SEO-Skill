# Search Intent: 6 practical levels

A finer intent model than the classic four (informational / commercial /
transactional / navigational). Classify the target query's intent, then match
**content format** to it. Misreading intent is the most expensive SEO mistake —
it attracts the wrong visitors.

| # | Intent level | User is… | Example query | Best-fit formats |
|---|--------------|----------|---------------|------------------|
| 1 | **Informational** | Learning a topic/concept | "what is X / how does X work" | Guides, blog posts, tutorials, explainers, FAQs |
| 2 | **Diagnostic** | Trying to identify why something is wrong / its cause | "why is my organic traffic dropping" | Troubleshooting guides, audits, diagnostic frameworks, how-to & problem→solution articles |
| 3 | **Exploratory** | Discovering available solutions (not ready to decide) | "ways to improve website visibility" | Solution guides, frameworks, strategy guides, overviews, topic hubs |
| 4 | **Comparative** | Evaluating alternatives before deciding | "best tool for SEO" / "X vs Y" | Comparisons, reviews, alternatives, feature breakdowns, pros & cons |
| 5 | **Transactional** | Ready to act — buy, sign up, hire | "hire an SEO consultant" | Landing pages, product pages, pricing pages, service pages, lead forms |
| 6 | **Navigational** | Looking for a specific site/brand/page | "<brand> login" | Homepage, login pages, brand pages, product/feature pages, support pages |

## Expand a query into its full journey

A 1–2 word head term hides a whole decision process. Map it before writing:

- **Pyramid of user needs** — explode a head term into its layered
  sub-considerations (e.g. "Ford Focus" → body style, fuel type, year, trim,
  features). Each layer is a query cluster you can own.
- **The "messy middle"** (Think with Google) — between trigger and purchase, users
  loop non-linearly between **exploration** and **evaluation**. Serve *both*
  phases, not just the transactional end.
- **Two modes of search** (Bing) — "I know exactly what I want" (navigational /
  simple informational → one precise answer page) vs. "I'm working through a
  process" (multi-iteration → comprehensive hub content). Decide which the query is.

## Tag intent with business value

When charting target keywords, tag each with **intent level + value-per-visitor +
an opportunity note** (demand vs. average traffic value). This turns intent from a
label into an explicit **prioritization** input — chase high-value intent first,
not just high-volume.

## Finding real query phrasings

- **Vocabulary spread** — experts and novices phrase the same need differently
  (acronym `[nlcs]` vs. `[baseball playoffs]`). Deliberately cover phrasings across
  expertise levels.
- **Autocomplete / PAA mining** — search-box autocomplete and "People Also Ask" are
  fast sources of long-tail and question phrasings.
- **Say it aloud** — speak the query as a person would ask it, and ask someone of a
  different demographic how they'd phrase it; capture the variants (voice &
  natural-language queries).

## Why long-tail breadth wins (benchmarks)

- ~58.8% of queries are 1–3 words; ~78.8% are 1–4 words.
- **~95% of all queries are searched ≤10 times/month** (Ahrefs) — the demand lives
  in the long tail, so topical breadth beats chasing a few head terms.
- Users who find useful **informational** content on your site are **~131% more
  likely to buy from you later** (Conductor) — justifies top-of-funnel investment.

## How to apply

1. Classify the primary query's intent level before writing or auditing a page.
2. Confirm the **page type matches** the intent (don't answer a comparative query
   with a thin product page, or a transactional query with a blog post).
3. Map each **cluster** across levels: an informational/diagnostic/exploratory hub
   feeding comparative and transactional pages (see `internal-linking.md`).
4. For AI search, lead each section with a **self-contained answer** so it can be
   cited regardless of intent level (see `content-and-geo.md`).
