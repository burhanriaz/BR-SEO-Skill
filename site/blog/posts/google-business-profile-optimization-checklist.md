Optimizing a Google Business Profile means filling in every field accurately and keeping it inside Google's guidelines: your real-world name, a real address or a hidden address with a service area, the most specific primary category, complete hours, and a link to the right page on your site. Most ranking gains come from completeness and accuracy; most losses come from breaking a rule that leads to suspension.

## Key takeaways

- The name, address and category fields carry the most risk. Get them right before touching anything else.
- Completeness helps. Google says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up in local results.
- Link each profile to its matching location page, tagged with UTM parameters so you can measure it.
- Q&A is gone. Since late 2025, Google's AI "Ask" feature answers questions from your profile, reviews and website, so those sources need to be complete.
- Re-check the profile after every real-world change: a move, new hours, a rebrand, a new phone system.

This checklist goes field by field. For how the profile fits into local ranking as a whole, start with the [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide-rank-in-google-maps-and-the-local-pack/).

## Before you start: ownership and verification

- [ ] **The profile is claimed and verified** by an account the business controls, not only by a former agency or employee.
- [ ] **At least two owners or managers** are on the profile, so access isn't lost when one person leaves.
- [ ] **There's one profile per location.** Search Maps for your name, phone number and old addresses. Duplicates split reviews and can lead to suspension; request removal or merging.

Google's help page on [improving local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) lists verification first, for a reason: unverified profiles are less likely to show up.

## Name

- [ ] **The name matches your real-world branding**: signage, website, invoices.
- [ ] **No keywords, cities, taglines, phone numbers or URLs** in the name.
- [ ] **No ALL-CAPS styling, store codes or trademark symbols** unless they're part of the real name.
- [ ] Legal suffixes like "LLC" only if you actually use them publicly.

Google's [guidelines for representing your business](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177) say "including unnecessary information in your business name isn't permitted." Keyword-stuffed names are one of the most common reasons for suspension, and competitors can suggest edits that strip them anyway.

| Real-world name | Acceptable profile name | Not acceptable |
|---|---|---|
| Harbor Dental | Harbor Dental | Harbor Dental – Best Dentist Portland |
| Lumen Coffee Roasters | Lumen Coffee Roasters | Lumen Coffee – Open 7am |
| Greenline Plumbing LLC (on signage) | Greenline Plumbing LLC | GREENLINE PLUMBING Emergency 24/7 |

## Address and service area

- [ ] **A precise street address** where the business actually operates, including the suite number.
- [ ] **No P.O. boxes or remote mailboxes.**
- [ ] **No virtual offices.** Google says a location you rent but don't operate from isn't eligible for a profile.
- [ ] **Service-area businesses hide the street address** and set a service area of cities or postal codes. Google says the area generally shouldn't extend farther than about two hours of driving from your base.
- [ ] **Hybrid businesses** (a storefront that also travels to customers) keep the address visible and add a service area.
- [ ] **The map pin sits on the right entrance.** Drag it if Google placed it on the wrong building.

Service-area businesses have a few extra rules, such as not creating a separate profile for each city they serve. One profile for the base, with a service area, is the model Google expects.

## Categories

- [ ] **The primary category is the most specific one** that completes "this business is a…". "Pediatric dentist" beats "dentist" if that's what you are.
- [ ] **Additional categories are few and accurate.** Only add what the business actually is, not what it has ("has Wi-Fi" is an attribute, not a category).
- [ ] **You've checked competitors.** Search your main term and note the primary categories of the top three profiles in the pack. If all of them use a more specific category that also fits you, consider it.
- [ ] **Categories aren't used as keywords.** Google's guidelines say not to.

The primary category is one of the strongest relevance signals you control. It also unlocks category-specific features, such as menus for restaurants or bookings for some service businesses.

## Contact details

- [ ] **Phone:** a local number under the business's direct control, preferred over a central call-center number. No premium-rate numbers.
- [ ] **Call tracking:** if you use a tracking number, the common approach is to make it the primary number and keep your main local number as an additional number, so the main number stays connected to the business across the web.
- [ ] **Website:** link to the page for this location. A single-location business can link to the home page; multi-location brands should link each profile to its own location page.
- [ ] **UTM tags** on the website link so profile traffic is separable in analytics:

```text
https://www.example.com/locations/portland/?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp
```

- [ ] **Booking or appointment links** go to a page that works on mobile.

## Hours

- [ ] **Regular hours** match what customers actually experience.
- [ ] **Holiday and special hours** are set ahead of time. Wrong holiday hours are an easy way to lose a customer and earn a bad review.
- [ ] **More hours** (pickup, delivery, senior hours, drive-through) are added where the category offers them.
- [ ] **Seasonal businesses** mark themselves temporarily closed during the off-season, as Google's guidelines suggest, instead of deleting hours.
- [ ] **By-appointment-only** businesses don't list hours that imply walk-ins.

## Services, products and description

- [ ] **Services:** list every real service, using Google's predefined options where they exist and custom services where they don't, each with a short plain description.
- [ ] **Products:** add your main products or product categories with prices where relevant. Retailers in eligible regions can also show in-store products.
- [ ] **Description:** a plain summary of what you do, who you serve and what makes you different. No links, no keyword lists, no promotions.
- [ ] **Opening date** is set.

## Attributes

- [ ] Accessibility attributes (wheelchair-accessible entrance, restroom, parking) are accurate.
- [ ] Service options (online appointments, onsite services, delivery) match reality.
- [ ] Identity attributes the owner chooses to show are set deliberately.
- [ ] Payment methods are listed.

Attributes help with refined searches and with AI answers about your business, since the Ask feature draws on profile information.

## Photos and video

- [ ] **Logo and cover photo** are uploaded and look right when cropped.
- [ ] **Exterior photos** from the street, so people recognize the building.
- [ ] **Interior, team and work photos** that show what customers actually get.
- [ ] **New photos added over time**, not all on day one.
- [ ] **No stock photos** or images with added text overlays pretending to be real photos.

## Posts and updates

- [ ] Offers, events and updates are posted when there's something real to say.
- [ ] Each post has a clear call to action and a link to the relevant page.
- [ ] Expired offers are removed or replaced.

## Reviews

- [ ] A routine asks **every** customer for a review using the profile's review link or QR code.
- [ ] Owners reply to reviews, positive and negative, briefly and politely.
- [ ] No gating, incentives, staff reviews or review swaps.

The rules here are strict and enforced. Google treats reviews from employees, relatives or anyone paid or rewarded for them as fake engagement and removes them.

## Questions customers ask

Business Profile Q&A was retired in late 2025. Google replaced it with an AI "Ask" feature in Maps that answers from your profile data, reviews and website. That changes the job:

- [ ] Your website answers the common questions plainly: parking, pricing ranges, insurance accepted, how long jobs take, areas served.
- [ ] Profile attributes and services cover the same facts.
- [ ] Old seeded Q&A content you relied on is moved to your website.

## Your website backs it up

- [ ] Name, address and phone on the website match the profile **exactly**, as text.
- [ ] The linked location page has valid `LocalBusiness` structured data that matches the visible details. [Structured data in 2026](/blog/structured-data-in-2026-which-schema-types-still-get-rich-results/) explains which types still produce rich results.
- [ ] The location page shows the same hours as the profile.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword-stuffed name | Suspension risk; edits suggested by others | Use the real name only |
| Virtual office address | Not eligible; verification fails or profile removed | Real staffed address or hidden address with service area |
| Wrong primary category | Weaker relevance for your main searches | Most specific category that completes "is a" |
| Profile links to the home page in a chain | Location relevance diluted | Link each profile to its location page |
| Holiday hours never set | Customers arrive to a closed door | Set special hours in advance |
| Only one owner on the account | Lost access when that person leaves | Add a second owner |
