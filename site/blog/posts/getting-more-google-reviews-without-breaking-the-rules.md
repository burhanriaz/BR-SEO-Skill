The way to get more Google reviews without breaking the rules is to ask every customer, at the moment they're most satisfied, with a direct review link, and to offer nothing in return. Google allows asking and reminding; it prohibits review gating, incentives of any kind, and reviews from anyone with a conflict of interest. A steady, ordinary process beats any shortcut, because shortcuts get reviews removed and profiles restricted.

## Key takeaways

- **Ask everyone.** Selectively asking happy customers is review gating, which Google prohibits.
- **Offer nothing.** No discounts, freebies, draws or payment, not even for changing a negative review.
- **No conflicts of interest.** Staff, owners, relatives, contractors and competitors shouldn't review the business.
- **Make it easy.** Use the review link or QR code from your Business Profile, sent at the right moment.
- **Reply to reviews.** Short, specific, polite replies to both positive and negative reviews.

## Why reviews matter for local SEO

Google's page on [improving your local ranking](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says prominence, how well known a business is, takes reviews into account, and that more reviews and positive ratings can improve local ranking. Reviews also do a second job: they're what searchers read when choosing between three businesses in the pack. And since late 2025, Google's AI "Ask" feature in Maps answers questions about your business partly from your reviews, so their content matters, not just the star average.

For where reviews sit among the other local factors, see the [local SEO guide](/blog/local-seo-guide-rank-in-google-maps-and-the-local-pack/).

## What Google's policy actually says

The rules live in Google's [prohibited and restricted content policy for Maps](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114). The relevant parts, in plain terms:

| Practice | Allowed? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Asking every customer for a review | Yes | Google explicitly allows asking, without incentives or influencing the content |
| Sending one reminder | Yes | Still a neutral request |
| Sharing your review link or QR code | Yes | Google's own [review guidance](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) recommends it |
| Asking only customers you think are happy | No | "Selectively solicit positive reviews" is prohibited |
| A "how did we do?" form that sends 5-star clicks to Google and others to a private form | No | That's review gating |
| A discount, free item or prize-draw entry for a review | No | Incentives of any kind are prohibited |
| Paying a customer to update or delete a negative review | No | Explicitly prohibited |
| Employees or the owner reviewing the business | No | Conflict of interest |
| Family members, former staff or contractors reviewing | No | Also conflicts of interest |
| Staff targets for a number of reviews, or reviews that mention specific words | No | Google prohibits soliciting a set number or specific content |
| Handing customers a tablet in-store to leave a review on the spot | No | Pressuring customers on the premises is prohibited |
| Review swaps with other businesses | No | Fake engagement |

The common thread: you can ask, but you can't select, reward, script or pressure.

### What happens if you break the rules

Google says fake engagement "is not allowed and will be removed." Beyond removing individual reviews, Google can restrict a profile it believes is being manipulated, for example by temporarily blocking new reviews or showing a notice to visitors that suspicious reviews were removed. That notice is far worse for conversions than a 4.3 average.

## A review request process that works

### 1. Pick the right moment

Ask when the customer has just experienced the result, not when they've just paid.

- A restaurant: as the bill is paid, or in a follow-up the same evening.
- A plumber: when the job is finished and tested.
- A dentist: after the appointment, by text or email.
- An online store with local pickup: a day after pickup.

### 2. Get your direct review link

In your Business Profile, use the option to ask for reviews to copy your short review link or download a QR code. The link opens the review box directly, which removes most of the friction.

### 3. Send a short, neutral request

Here's a hypothetical example of a message that stays inside the rules:

```text
Hi Sam, thanks for choosing Greenline Plumbing today.
If you have a minute, we'd appreciate an honest review on Google:
https://g.page/r/EXAMPLE/review
It helps neighbors find a plumber they can trust. – Priya, Greenline
```

Notice what's missing: no request for five stars, no suggested wording, no offer, and no screening question first.

### 4. Ask everyone

Build the request into your normal workflow so it goes to every customer automatically: a CRM trigger, a line on the receipt, a QR code on the invoice. Automation is the easiest way to avoid accidental gating, because nobody is deciding who gets asked.

### 5. One reminder, then stop

A single follow-up a few days later is reasonable. Repeated nagging annoys customers and edges toward pressure.

## Where to put the review link

- Email and SMS follow-ups after a job or appointment.
- Receipts and invoices (printed QR code or link).
- A card handed over at the end of a visit, so the customer can review later, at home.
- Your website's contact or thank-you pages.
- Email signatures of customer-facing staff.

Avoid placing a "Review us" kiosk or tablet at the counter for customers to use on the spot.

## How to reply to reviews

Google's [guidance on reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122) asks for replies that are professional, polite and brief, without promotional content. A practical pattern:

**For positive reviews:** thank the person by name, mention something specific from their review, and keep it to two or three sentences. Avoid pasting the same thank-you on every review.

**For negative reviews:**

1. Reply promptly and calmly.
2. Acknowledge the specific problem without arguing.
3. Don't reveal private details about the customer.
4. Say what you've done or will do, and offer a way to continue offline.
5. Sign with a name.

A hypothetical example:

```text
Hi Jordan, I'm sorry the technician arrived two hours late without calling.
That's not acceptable, and we've changed how we notify customers about delays.
I'd like to make this right; please call me directly at 555-0142. – Priya, owner
```

Google itself notes that a mix of positive and negative feedback often feels more trustworthy to readers. A thoughtful reply to a 2-star review can do more for conversions than another 5-star review.

### Flagging reviews that break the rules

You can flag a review that violates Google's content policy (spam, off-topic, conflict of interest, offensive content). A review being negative, or one you disagree with, isn't a violation. Don't offer anything for a change or removal; that's prohibited.

## Reviews on your website

You can show reviews on your site, but don't add `Review` or `AggregateRating` structured data for reviews of your own business. Google's review snippet guidelines treat that as self-serving, and it isn't eligible for star rich results. [Structured data in 2026](/blog/structured-data-in-2026-which-schema-types-still-get-rich-results/) covers which markup still pays off. Showing real reviews as plain content, with a link to your Google profile, is fine.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| A "rate us 1–5" screen that sends only 4–5 to Google | Send everyone the same Google review link |
| "10% off your next visit for a review" | Remove the offer; ask plainly |
| Staff asked to collect 20 reviews a month | Track requests sent, not reviews received |
| Asking employees and friends to "get things started" | Start with real customers only |
| Buying reviews from a vendor | Never; they're removed and risk a profile restriction |
| Ignoring negative reviews | Reply to every negative review within a few days |
| Copy-paste replies | Short, specific replies that reference the review |

## A simple monthly review routine

1. Check that the request goes to every customer (compare requests sent with jobs completed).
2. Reply to every new review.
3. Read the negative reviews for operational problems worth fixing.
4. Flag reviews that clearly break the policy.
5. Note recurring questions in reviews and answer them on your website and profile, since the Ask feature draws on both.
