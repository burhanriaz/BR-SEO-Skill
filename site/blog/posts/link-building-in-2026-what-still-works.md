Link building in 2026 works when you create something other sites genuinely want to cite, then put it in front of the people who write about your topic. Editorial links from relevant sites still matter because Google still uses links to understand pages, but any link you pay for, trade or automate without qualifying it is link spam. The work that pays off is slower and more specific than the old playbooks: fewer links, from the right places, for real reasons.

Key takeaways:

- Links still matter. Google says PageRank "continues to be part of our core ranking systems," and links remain the main way it discovers new pages.
- The unit that counts is the unique, relevant referring domain, not the raw link count.
- Earn links with linkable assets (data, tools, definitive references) plus targeted, honest outreach.
- Paid, exchanged or automated links must carry `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"`, or they violate Google's link spam policy.
- Measure progress with referring-domain growth, links to the pages you meant to promote, and brand mentions, not a vendor authority score.

## Do links still matter?

Yes, but in a narrower way than ten years ago. Google's [ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide) says its link analysis systems "understand how pages link to each other as a way to determine what pages are about," and that PageRank is still one of the core systems. The [SEO starter guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) adds that "the vast majority of the new pages Google finds every day are through links."

What changed is how much of the manipulation Google can now ignore. Its December 2022 link spam update used SpamBrain to [neutralize unnatural links](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/12/december-22-link-spam-update), both from sites that buy links and from sites that exist to sell them. When those links lose their value, whatever ranking credit they passed is simply gone. So a profile padded with junk is not just risky. A lot of it does nothing at all.

The practical reading is this: a link helps when a real site, on a related topic, chose to point readers at you because your page was the best thing to point at. Everything in a modern link plan should lead to more of those moments.

## What a valuable link looks like

Before you spend a week on outreach, agree on what you are trying to earn. The table below is the rubric br-web-seo uses when it reads a backlink export.

| Signal | Worth more | Worth less or risky |
|--------|-----------|--------------------|
| Choice | An editor chose to link | You placed it, paid for it or swapped for it |
| Relevance | The linking page covers your topic | Off-topic or unrelated niche |
| Placement | In the body, in context | Sitewide footer, sidebar, author bio |
| Anchor text | Brand, URL or natural phrase | The same exact-match keyword at scale |
| Diversity | Many different referring domains | Many links from a handful of domains |
| Attribute | Followed editorial link | Paid link without `sponsored` or `nofollow` |

Use **unique referring domains** as the gut-check metric. Ten relevant domains that each linked once usually say more about your authority than five hundred links from one partner's footer.

## What no longer works (and why)

Google's [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies#link-spam) define link spam as "creating links to or from a site primarily for the purpose of manipulating search rankings." The examples listed there cover most of what used to be sold as link building:

- Buying or selling links that pass ranking credit, including trading goods, services or free products for a link.
- Excessive link exchanges and partner pages built only for cross-linking.
- Automated programs or services that create links.
- Advertorials, native ads, guest posts or press releases where payment buys followed links or optimized anchors.
- Low-quality directory and bookmark links.
- Keyword-rich links hidden in widgets, footers or templates distributed across many sites.
- Forum comments with optimized links in the post or signature.

Two related policies close the other loopholes. Expired domain abuse covers buying lapsed domains to reuse their old authority for unrelated content. Private blog networks fall under the general link scheme rule however they are dressed up.

None of this means you can never pay for exposure. Sponsorships, ads and affiliate placements are fine when the links are qualified. Google says buying and selling links for advertising is not a violation if the links use `rel="nofollow"` or `rel="sponsored"`. The attribute is what separates advertising from a link scheme.

```html
<!-- Paid placement or sponsorship: qualified, so it is allowed -->
<a href="https://example.com/tool" rel="sponsored">Example's crawl tool</a>

<!-- Editorial link you earned: no rel needed -->
<a href="https://example.com/research/crawl-study">their 2026 crawl study</a>
```

## The tactics that still earn links

Every method below works for the same reason: it gives a writer something they need. Pick two or three that fit your resources rather than trying all of them at once.

### 1. Linkable assets

A linkable asset is a page that people reference instead of rewrite. The formats that attract links reliably are the ones that save someone else work:

- **Original data**: a survey, a benchmark, an analysis of public data, or anonymized aggregate data from your own product.
- **Free tools and calculators**: a checker, a generator, a cost estimator.
- **Definitive references**: a glossary, a spec explainer, a comparison table that is kept current.
- **Templates and checklists** people can copy into their own work.

Google's helpful-content guidance asks whether content provides "original information, reporting, research, or analysis" ([source](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). That question is also the best predictor of whether anyone will link to an asset. If a page only restates what ranks already, nobody needs to cite you.

### 2. Link-intersect prospecting

The warmest prospects are sites that already link to two or more of your competitors but not to you. They cover your topic and they link out. The method takes an afternoon with any backlink tool export, and it tells you which asset to build because you can see what earned each competitor's link. The full walkthrough is in the [link-intersect post](/blog/link-intersect-find-sites-that-link-to-competitors-but-not-you/).

### 3. Digital PR

Digital PR means becoming a source journalists quote. It usually pairs an original data asset with a short, newsworthy finding and a pitch to reporters who cover that beat. It also produces brand mentions without links, which still build recognition and are increasingly what AI answers draw on (see [how to get cited by AI search](/blog/generative-engine-optimization-geo-how-to-get-cited-by-ai-search/)).

### 4. Link reclamation

You probably already have lost links. Pages that moved without a redirect, renamed product URLs and deleted blog posts can all still receive links from other sites. Find old URLs that return 404 but have inbound links, then 301 them to the closest live equivalent. It is usually the highest-return, lowest-effort link work on an established site.

### 5. Unlinked mentions

Search for your brand, product and founder names. When a page mentions you without linking, and a link would help its readers (for example, they name your tool but readers cannot find it), a short note asking for the link is reasonable. Many will say no. That is fine; the mention still has value.

### 6. Resource pages and expert contributions

Curated resource lists, university guides, association pages and industry roundups link out by design. If your asset is genuinely one of the best on the subject, suggest it. Expert quotes for other people's articles and genuine guest contributions also work, as long as the purpose is to inform the audience and you do not pay for followed, keyword-rich anchors.

## A 90-day link plan

Here is a hypothetical example of how a small team could sequence the work. Adjust the scope to your capacity.

| Weeks | Work | Output |
|-------|------|--------|
| 1–2 | Export your and 3–5 competitors' referring domains; run link-intersect; run a broken-backlink check | Prospect sheet, list of URLs to 301 |
| 2–3 | Ship the redirects; fix internal links to the money pages you want to lift | Reclaimed links; authority flows to the right pages |
| 3–6 | Build one linkable asset matched to the most common link reason in your prospect list | One data study, tool or reference |
| 6–10 | Outreach to 40–80 hand-picked prospects; pitch the data to relevant reporters | Replies, links, mentions |
| 10–12 | Review: new referring domains, which pitches worked, which pages gained | Decision on the next asset |

Keep the prospect sheet simple. These columns are enough:

| Column | What goes in it |
|--------|-----------------|
| Domain | Registrable domain, normalized |
| Linking page | The exact URL that links to competitors |
| Why they link | Resource list, data citation, review, tool mention |
| Contact | A named person, not a generic inbox if you can avoid it |
| Asset to pitch | Your page that fits the reason they link |
| Status | Not contacted, sent, follow-up, linked, declined |
| Notes | Anything personal and relevant from their work |

## Outreach that respects the recipient

Most outreach fails because it is about the sender. A good email shows you read the page, explains why your resource helps their readers, and makes saying yes easy. One follow-up is plenty.

```text
Subject: A current source for your [topic] guide

Hi [Name],

I read your guide on [topic] while researching [specific thing]. The
section on [specific point] was useful, especially [detail].

You cite [old source] for [claim]. We published updated data on the same
question last month, based on [method in one line]:
[URL]

If it's useful for your readers, feel free to use it. Either way, thanks
for keeping the guide up to date.

[Your name], [role] at [company]
```

What to leave out: offers of money, products or a reciprocal link in exchange for the link, requests for a specific keyword anchor, and fake familiarity. Any of those can turn an editorial link into a scheme, and all of them lower your reply rate.

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing a third-party authority score.** Domain ratings and trust scores are vendor estimates. Use them to sort a list, never as the goal.
- **Pointing every link at the home page.** Links should land on the page that deserves them, and your internal links should then route that authority to the pages that convert.
- **Over-optimized anchors.** If you can influence the anchor at all, let it be your brand or a natural description.
- **Ignoring internal links.** A new link to a page buried five clicks deep, with no internal links in, does less than it could. Internal linking is the multiplier for everything above.
- **Disavowing out of fear.** Random spam links you did not build are normally ignored. The disavow tool is for manual actions and schemes you or a past agency created.
- **Building, then not maintaining.** Assets with stale data stop earning links. Put a refresh date on every data page.

## How to measure link building

Report on what you can verify. Search Console's [Links report](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/9049606) shows top linking sites, top linked pages and top linking text from Google's own sample. Pair it with one backlink tool export for trend lines.

Track monthly:

- New unique referring domains, filtered for relevance.
- Links pointing at the specific assets and pages you promoted.
- Lost links worth reclaiming.
- Unlinked brand mentions found.
- Rankings and clicks for the pages that received links, compared with similar pages that did not.

Avoid reporting raw link counts as success. A spike from one sitewide footer looks great on a chart and means very little.

## Where links fit in a full audit

Links are one input among many. A page that is not indexable, renders blank without JavaScript or answers the wrong intent will not be rescued by backlinks. Run the technical and content checks first so the links you earn land on pages that can use them.
