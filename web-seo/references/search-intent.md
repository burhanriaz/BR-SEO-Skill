# Search Intent: 6 practical levels

A finer intent model than the classic four (informational / commercial /
transactional / navigational). Classify the target query's intent, then match
**content format** to it. Misreading intent is the most expensive SEO mistake —
it attracts the wrong visitors.

| # | Intent level | User is… | Example query | Best-fit formats |
|---|--------------|----------|---------------|------------------|
| 1 | **Informational** | Learning a topic/concept | "what is X / how does X work" | Guides, blog posts, tutorials, explainers, FAQs |
| 2 | **Diagnostic** | Trying to identify why something is wrong / its cause | "why is my organic traffic dropping" | Troubleshooting guides, audits, diagnostic frameworks, how-to & problem→solution articles |
| 3 | **Exploratory** | Discovering available solutions (not ready to decide) | "ways to improve website visibility" | Solution guides, frameworks, strategy guides, overviews, topic hubs |
| 4 | **Comparative** | Evaluating alternatives before deciding | "best tool for SEO" / "X vs Y" | Comparisons, reviews, alternatives, feature breakdowns, pros & cons |
| 5 | **Transactional** | Ready to act — buy, sign up, hire | "hire an SEO consultant" | Landing pages, product pages, pricing pages, service pages, lead forms |
| 6 | **Navigational** | Looking for a specific site/brand/page | "<brand> login" | Homepage, login pages, brand pages, product/feature pages, support pages |

## How to apply

1. Classify the primary query's intent level before writing or auditing a page.
2. Confirm the **page type matches** the intent (don't answer a comparative query
   with a thin product page, or a transactional query with a blog post).
3. Map each **cluster** across levels: an informational/diagnostic/exploratory hub
   feeding comparative and transactional pages (see `internal-linking.md`).
4. For AI search, lead each section with a **self-contained answer** so it can be
   cited regardless of intent level (see `content-and-geo.md`).
